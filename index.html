Voice isn't a vibe.It's a set of choices.

The real problem
"Warm but professional" means nothing without showing what it looks like when the website goes down, the review is negative, and the cold email needs to land.
The brands with strong voices made specific choices explicitly — about contractions, about first person, about sentence length, about which words they never use and which they always reach for. The brands that sound inconsistent never made those choices at all. Or made them once in a brand guidelines document nobody reads. The Brand Voice Builder makes the choices explicit — and then shows what they look like applied to the twelve situations every brand actually writes for.

Tool 01
Brand Voice Guide
A complete, usable voice guide — not a mood board or a set of adjectives, but a document that tells anyone writing for this brand exactly what choices to make. Six sections covering everything from personality to punctuation.
Voice Overview
2–3 paragraphs on what the brand sounds like and why — specific, not descriptive
Voice Attributes
4 attributes with spectrum bars — Formal/Casual, Minimal/Expansive, etc. Measurable, not just descriptive
Brand as a Person
Who would this brand be? Makes the voice feel real rather than theoretical
Do This, Not That
5 specific dos, 5 specific donts — checkable rules, not vague principles
Word Choices
8 words/phrases to use, 8 to avoid — specific to this brand and positioning
Grammar & Style
5 specific rules on punctuation, sentence length, formatting, capitalisation
Tool 02
Messaging Framework
Core brand messages per audience segment. A productivity-focused professional and a gift buyer need different angles of the same brand story. The framework writes both — so whoever is writing for each audience starts with the right message, not a generic one.
Brand foundation
Tagline, mission, and full positioning statement for the overarching brand
Primary audience
Audience insight + 4 messages: primary, two supporting, and CTA
Secondary audience
Different angle of the same story — relevant to what this audience cares about
Tertiary audience
Optional third segment — B2B gifting, investors, press, or any other audience
Tool 03
Tone of Voice Examples
Before/after in up to 12 writing contexts — each showing an off-brand version (realistic, not a strawman) and an on-brand version with a note on exactly what the on-brand version does differently. The abstract made concrete.
Off-brand version
Realistic — the copy a brand actually writes when not being intentional. Generic, functional, forgettable.
On-brand version
The same context in the specific voice — different in ways that are measurable and repeatable
Why it works
One sentence naming what the on-brand version does differently — the rule, not just the result
